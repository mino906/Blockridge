📖 The Construction Guide

BuildRight — Foundation Lesson

What is a Foundation?

A foundation is the lowest part of a building. It sits below the ground and transfers the weight of the entire structure safely into the earth. Without a solid foundation, a building will sink, tilt, or crack over time.

Why Do We Dig Down?

The top layer of soil is soft and unstable — it shifts with rain, tree roots, and temperature changes. Builders dig below this layer to reach stable, load-bearing soil or rock. In cold climates, foundations must also go below the frost line — the depth at which the ground freezes — because frozen ground expands and can lift or crack a shallow foundation.

What Blocks to Use

  • Concrete — extremely strong under compression. It holds up the weight of walls, floors, and roof above.
  • Iron / Rebar — steel bars embedded in concrete. Concrete is brittle under tension (pulling forces). Rebar handles tension so the foundation doesn't crack.
